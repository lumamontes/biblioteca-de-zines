# Academic studies on zines, archives, metadata, and creator autonomy

Research date: 2026-09-24

## Scope and evidence standard

This is a selective review of peer-reviewed articles, an open conference/journal
paper, and a Brazilian undergraduate thesis. Priority was given to publisher or
institutional-repository full text, DOI records, and stable open-access copies.
The findings below distinguish what was read in full text from what is supported
only by an official record or abstract. This file complements the repository's
broader notes on books, zine-library practice, Brazilian collections, and
ZineCore2; it does not repeat those sources as if they were empirical studies.

The implications are recommendations for Biblioteca's catalogue and archive
model, not claims that any source prescribes Biblioteca's exact schema.

## Findings at a glance

The studies converge on five practical conclusions:

1. A zine's meaning is inseparable from its material form, production process,
   circulation, and social position. A title/creator/year record is not enough.
2. Zines can be both archival evidence and an archival process. Co-creation,
   reminiscence, shared stewardship, and community interpretation can be part of
   what the publication is and how it should be described.
3. Description is an intervention, not a neutral mirror. Classification and
   correction can make marginal material findable while also imposing language,
   exposing identities, or erasing creator terms.
4. Access policy is part of representation. Open stacks, public display, web
   indexing, downloads, preservation copies, and reuse permissions are distinct
   decisions.
5. Brazilian scholarship confirms that fanzine description needs contextual and
   discourse-sensitive treatment, but the available Brazilian evidence is not a
   ready-made library metadata standard. Portuguese-language terminology and
   creator wording should therefore be preserved alongside normalized access
   points, not replaced by them.

## Studies

### 1. Zavam, Aurea Suely (2006)

**Verified citation:** Zavam, Aurea Suely. "FANZINE: A PLURIVALENCIA
PARATOPICA." *Linguagem em (Dis)curso* 6, no. 1 (2006): 9-28.
https://doi.org/10.1590/1982-4017-06-01-01

**Access and verification:** The article is open access in SciELO. The full
Portuguese HTML text was inspected, including the article's method, examples,
conclusion, and references: [SciELO full text](https://www.scielo.br/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1518-76322006000100009&tlng=pt).
Crossref confirms the DOI, journal, volume, issue, pages, author, and original
English title.

**Research question and method:** The article asks how the fanzine relates to
the discourse-analysis concept of *paratopia*. It uses French discourse
analysis, especially the relationship between genre, support, enunciation,
positioning, ethos, and circulation. Its corpus includes literary fanzines and
close readings of examples, including their covers, internal sections, and
poetic/discursive positioning.

**Findings:** Zavam argues that a fanzine should not be reduced to a textual
genre or physical support. It is better understood as an enunciative device in
which form, language, social position, material production, and diffusion are
mutually implicated. The fanzine's marginal or independent position is not just
background context: the constraints and visible marks of production can become
part of its meaning. The conclusion describes a plural form of *paratopia*:
the maker's social position, the place from which the publication speaks, and
the alternative device/circulation route all matter. The article also warns
that this status is historical rather than permanent; a device considered
marginal can be reclassified as its social position changes.

**Limitations:** This is a discourse-analysis study of literary fanzines, not a
cataloguing experiment or representative survey of Brazilian fanzines. Its
conceptual vocabulary should not be turned into universal subject headings.
The article's 2006 examples predate current digital publishing practices.

**Metadata/cataloguing implications for Biblioteca:**

- Keep `zine format/form`, `reproduction method`, `language`, `description`,
  `circulation context`, and `place` as distinct assertions; a single genre
  field cannot carry the publication's whole communicative situation.
- Record material or production evidence when it is supplied or observed,
  including photocopy, mimeograph, collage, legibility, folded assembly, and
  other features that affect interpretation. Do not infer those facts from a
  filename or PDF alone.
- Add role-bearing context for creation, publication, circulation, event, and
  community rather than treating one place as the publication's complete
  geography.
- Preserve the maker's own description of the publication beside any controlled
  form or subject mapping. A cataloguer's normalized label must not erase the
  publication's self-positioning.

### 2. Zauith, Davanco, and Barbieri (2019)

**Verified citation:** Zauith, Gabriella, Angelo Rogerio Davanco, and Marisa
Ramos Barbieri. "Oficina de fanzine." *Comunicacao & Educacao* 24, no. 1
(2019): 56-68. https://doi.org/10.11606/issn.2316-9125.v24i1p56-68

**Access and verification:** Crossref and the official USP journal record
confirm authorship, date, volume, issue, pages, DOI, and an official PDF link:
[USP record](https://www.revistas.usp.br/comueduc/article/view/150423).
The source is openly distributed by the journal. The accessible article record
and abstract were inspected; field-level details below are intentionally limited
to what the article reports there.

**Research question and method:** The article presents the Casa da Ciencia
fanzine workshop as an educommunication practice. Workshops led by journalists
ran in 2017-2018 within the Adote um Cientista program for primary and secondary
students. Students planned themes, wrote text, made illustrations, and
finished more than 100 fanzines.

**Findings:** The workshop gave students authorship of texts and illustrations,
supported planning and scientific communication, and enabled them to translate
scientific concepts for a general audience. The production context is therefore
not incidental: the educational setting, participants, roles, and many resulting
publications are part of the record of what was made.

**Limitations:** This is a report of one educational project, not a persistent
catalogue of all produced zines and not a controlled study of long-term learning
outcomes. Article authorship is not the same as authorship of each student-made
zine. The article's journal pagination must not be copied as the physical extent
of a zine.

**Metadata/cataloguing implications for Biblioteca:**

- Separate article authors, workshop facilitators, participating creators, and
  the educational program as contextual role assertions.
- Support production-event and workshop relationships, including date, place,
  prompt/program, and participant consent/visibility, without forcing all of
  that context into a subject string.
- Allow multiple zines to be submitted as a project set while preserving an
  individual publication record for each issue or finished object.
- Record creator-supplied authorship and unresolved authorship separately. A
  teacher, editor, or submitter must not become the creator by default.

### 3. Baker and Cantillon (2022)

**Verified citation:** Baker, Sarah, and Zelmarie Cantillon. "Zines as community
archive." *Archival Science* 22, no. 4 (2022): 539-561.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10502-022-09388-1

**Access and verification:** Crossref identifies the article as published open
access under CC BY and links the published HTML and PDF. The official record
states the research ethics approval and identifies the case publication. The
article's official full-text links are [HTML](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10502-022-09388-1/fulltext.html) and [PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10502-022-09388-1.pdf).

**Research question and method:** The article asks whether zines and zine-making
can function as community archives, rather than merely being objects collected
by archives. It examines *See You at the Paradise*, a zine co-created with
Norfolk Island community members in a research project about Kingston and
Arthur's Vale Historic Area. It applies six community-archive principles from
Michelle Caswell: participation, shared stewardship, multiplicity, activism,
reflexivity, and valuing affect.

**Findings:** The authors propose collaborative reminiscence as a seventh
principle. Zine-making operates as both product and process: it can strengthen
marginalized voices in dominant historical narratives, support community
building, and enable political resistance. This makes the zine's participants,
memories, editorial choices, and collaborative process relevant archival context,
not merely provenance notes hidden from readers.

**Limitations:** The study is a focused case study of one co-created zine and
one community/research project. Its principles are interpretive rather than a
validated metadata standard. The Norfolk Island setting and heritage-project
relationship will not transfer unchanged to every zine community.

**Metadata/cataloguing implications for Biblioteca:**

- Model the publication and its production/community process separately but link
  them explicitly: project, event, participants, editors, oral-history or
  reminiscence source, and publication.
- Preserve multiplicity: different participant accounts may coexist without
  requiring one authoritative summary to replace them.
- Record shared stewardship and contributor review as provenance/processing
  evidence, not as a claim that the archive owns or speaks for the community.
- Add visibility and consent decisions to participant, place, memory, and
  description assertions. A community archive relationship does not authorize
  unrestricted web indexing or reuse.
- Preserve a creator/community account of the zine's significance alongside a
  cataloguer summary and controlled subject terms.

### 4. Gordon, Hanna, Hoyer, and Ordaz (2016)

**Verified citation:** Gordon, Bonnie, Lani Hanna, Jen Hoyer, and Vero Ordaz.
"Archives, Education, and Access: Learning at Interference Archive."
*Radical Teacher* 105 (2016): 54-60. https://doi.org/10.5195/rt.2016.273

**Access and verification:** The official University of Pittsburgh journal page
provides the full PDF, citation, DOI, and license: [journal record](https://radicalteacher.library.pitt.edu/ojs/radicalteacher/article/view/273).
The official record and PDF link were checked; the findings below stay within
the access model and claims stated by the article rather than inferring results
from an abstract beyond its scope.

**Research question and method:** This is a reflective practice and community
archive account of how Interference Archive in Brooklyn provides access and
education. It describes an open-stacks policy and experiential learning, using
the archive's operating practice to examine how visitors, donors, and volunteers
participate in telling and reusing social-movement histories.

**Findings:** Access policy changes the educational and representational work an
archive can do. Open stacks and participatory learning make visitors and
contributors active interpreters rather than passive consumers. The authors
present this as a way to challenge institutional control over how movements are
represented and how archival material is reused.

**Limitations:** This is a situated practice account, not a comparative study
or evaluation of all open-stacks models. It concerns a physical community
archive and does not settle how web indexing, remote downloads, sensitive records,
or digital preservation should be handled.

**Metadata/cataloguing implications for Biblioteca:**

- Treat access as several dimensions: discovery, reading, download,
  preservation, replication, and reuse. `Published` or `public URL` must not
  mean permitted for every action.
- Record holdings, custody, condition, and access decisions separately from the
  publication description and from a digital file.
- Provide a correction/annotation route so community members can participate in
  description without needing to become cataloguers or disclose an offline
  identity.
- Preserve collection-level access policy and its revision date; do not infer
  that a collection-wide open-stacks principle applies identically to every
  publication or contributor.

### 5. Caswell and Cifor (2016)

**Verified citation:** Caswell, Michelle, and Marika Cifor. "From Human Rights
to Feminist Ethics: Radical Empathy in the Archives." *Archivaria* 81 (2016):
23-43. [Official journal record and PDF](https://archivaria.ca/index.php/archivaria/article/view/13557).

**Access and verification:** The official *Archivaria* page confirms the
authors, issue, pages, publication date, abstract, PDF link, and copyright
conditions. The source is scholarly and openly linked by the journal. The
discussion below is bounded to the article's stated theoretical argument and
its four relationship shifts; it is not presented as an empirical zine study.

**Research question and method:** The article asks what ethical framework should
guide archival social-justice work. It contrasts a legalistic rights framework
with feminist ethics of care and proposes radical empathy as a way to understand
relationships among creators, subjects, users, archivists, and communities.

**Findings:** Archivists are not detached custodians but caregivers tied to
records and people through mutual affective responsibility. The article proposes
four interrelated shifts in archival relationships, moving practice toward
attention to affect, embodied relationships, and responsibility rather than
only abstract individual rights. Its contribution is ethical and theoretical:
the people represented in records are part of the archival relationship, not
just data subjects.

**Limitations:** This is a theoretical article, not a field trial or metadata
benchmark, and it addresses archives generally rather than zines specifically.
"Empathy" cannot replace explicit consent, access controls, documentation, or
legal review.

**Metadata/cataloguing implications for Biblioteca:**

- Attach source, visibility, consent, uncertainty, and correction history to
  assertions and relationships, not only to the record as a whole.
- Treat a creator's request for anonymity, suppression, chosen name, or changed
  display as an archival-care concern even where a cataloguer could technically
  identify the person.
- Separate legal-rights claims from creator copying requests, Biblioteca's
  operational permission, and reader access decisions.
- Design review workflows around affected people and communities, while keeping
  an auditable record of what was changed, by whom, when, and on what evidence.

### 6. Drabinski (2013)

**Verified citation:** Drabinski, Emily. "Queering the Catalog: Queer Theory and
the Politics of Correction." *The Library Quarterly* 83, no. 2 (2013): 94-111.
https://doi.org/10.1086/669547

**Access and verification:** The published citation is confirmed by Crossref.
An accepted manuscript is available through CUNY Academic Works:
[repository record](https://academicworks.cuny.edu/gc_pubs/577/). The repository
record provides the full manuscript PDF and its abstract; the article's argument
was reviewed through that repository copy.

**Research question and method:** The article brings queer theory into dialogue
with library classification and cataloguing. It is a critical/theoretical
analysis rather than a user survey or controlled metadata experiment. It asks
what it means to correct biased knowledge-organization systems when categories
and identities remain historically contingent and contested.

**Findings:** Cataloguing and controlled vocabularies are not neutral systems
that can be corrected once and for all. A queer approach treats classification
as ideological and asks how marginalized users can locate themselves without
assuming that a single authorized vocabulary resolves the problem. Drabinski
shifts some responsibility from cataloguers who supply a final technical fix to
public-service and pedagogical work that helps users read and challenge the
catalog as a constructed text.

**Limitations:** The article is about queer theory and library catalogues in
general, not a study of zine records or Brazilian users. It does not prescribe
an implementation model for creator-controlled metadata.

**Metadata/cataloguing implications for Biblioteca:**

- Keep creator/community wording alongside controlled subjects; do not silently
  overwrite a local term when adding a normalized mapping.
- Version vocabulary changes and preserve historical terms/mappings so the
  record's descriptive history remains legible.
- Treat `unknown`, `anonymous`, `pseudonymous`, `private`, and `unresolved` as
  different evidence states; do not force a guessed authorized identity.
- Provide public explanatory context and user feedback/correction paths rather
  than presenting search categories as objective descriptions of communities.

### 7. Silva (2019), Brazilian thesis

**Verified citation:** Silva, Felipe de Jesus Rodrigues da. *Catalogacao de
publicacoes alternativas: um estudo sobre o fanzine e o cordel*. Undergraduate
thesis, Universidade de Brasilia, 2019. [BDM/UnB repository record](https://bdm.unb.br/handle/10483/22374).

**Access and verification:** The institutional repository record verifies the
author, title, institution, year, and persistent handle. The repository was
not reliably retrievable for the complete thesis during this review, so no
field-level recommendation or finding is attributed to the thesis here.

**Research question/method and findings:** The title and repository metadata
establish a Brazilian study of cataloguing alternative publications, explicitly
including fanzine and cordel. The research design, corpus, findings, and
proposed fields remain to be verified from the thesis text.

**Limitations:** This is an undergraduate thesis, not peer-reviewed journal
research or a national cataloguing code. The record alone cannot support claims
about its method or conclusions.

**Metadata/cataloguing implication for Biblioteca:** Treat this as a high-value
Portuguese-language research lead and vocabulary source, not as policy evidence.
Obtain and read the full thesis before adopting any field, classification,
ABNT/RDA interpretation, or fanzine-cordel analogy. Retain the thesis handle as
a research-source identifier and keep its verification state explicit.

## Cross-study design implications

### Publication and context

Represent a publication separately from its production event, workshop, project,
community archive, collection/series, holding, physical copy, digital asset,
and derivative. Link them with typed relationships. A workshop, archive, or
research article can explain a zine without becoming its creator or rights-holder.

### Agents and creator autonomy

Use contextual role assertions for creator, editor, illustrator, publisher,
facilitator, participant, submitter, archive steward, and rights claimant. Keep
public display name, pseudonym, collective name, anonymity, and unresolved
identity distinct. Never infer a legal identity from recognition, a social link,
article authorship, or an archive's research.

### Description and vocabulary

Preserve at least:

- title as presented and any normalized search title;
- creator-supplied description and cataloguer summary as separate values;
- language of publication/content and language of metadata/transcription where
  relevant;
- form/genre, subject, delivery format, and reproduction method as separate
  dimensions;
- local/community terms beside controlled mappings, with vocabulary version and
  source;
- date and place assertions with role, precision, uncertainty, and evidence.

### Materiality and evidence

Record physical extent, dimensions, binding/assembly, reproduction process,
folds, inserts, handwriting, collage, and other meaningful features when
observed or creator-supplied. Keep digital file page count, format, checksum,
scan properties, and derivative relation on the file asset/version. Do not infer
physical publication facts from a PDF preview.

### Access, rights, and care

Separate discovery, reading, downloading, preservation, replication, and reuse.
Preserve an original copying or anti-copyright statement verbatim with source,
but do not turn it automatically into permission to digitize or create
derivatives. Scope every rights/access decision to a publication, issue, copy,
file, page, or derivative. Provide correction, suppression, withdrawal, and
chosen-name workflows that do not require a pseudonymous creator to reveal a
legal identity.

### Provenance and accountability

Every normalized or sensitive assertion should be traceable to a source, actor,
date, visibility decision, and confidence/verification state. Community review
and shared stewardship should be recorded as provenance and participation, not
silently converted into institutional ownership. Public records should be
projections from richer internal evidence, not the only place where uncertainty,
privacy, and consent decisions exist.

## Overall limitations

- Zine-specific peer-reviewed research remains small and is concentrated in
  English-language US, UK, and Australian contexts.
- The Brazilian sources located here include one open full-text discourse study,
  one official journal source with workshop research, and a thesis whose full
  text still needs retrieval. This is useful regional evidence, not a Brazilian
  national standard.
- Several studies are interpretive case studies or practice accounts. They
  illuminate ethical and descriptive problems but do not statistically validate
  a universal schema.
- Community participation and creator autonomy can conflict with discovery,
  preservation, and interoperability. The catalogue needs explicit, revisable
  decisions rather than a single claim that a record is public or private.
- Rights and consent remain publication- and context-specific. Scholarly
  arguments about care or community stewardship do not themselves grant
  permission to host, index, reproduce, or transform a zine.
