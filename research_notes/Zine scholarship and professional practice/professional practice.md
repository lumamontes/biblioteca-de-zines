# Zine librarianship and professional practice

Research completed 2026-09-24. This note prioritizes first-party documentation written or maintained by named zine librarians, archivists, cataloguers, volunteers, and collecting institutions. “Guidance” below is a paraphrase of the cited source, not a claim that every institution follows one universal standard.

## Core professional guidance

### Names, anonymity, pseudonyms, and creator language

- The Code of Ethics says to use the form of name that appears on the piece. If reconciling it to an authority file, cataloguers must identify sensitive cases where the creator may not want a legal name associated with the zine. Authority records should contain no more personal information than necessary. Be prepared to change or remove name information at the creator's request. ([Code of Ethics, Organization 2](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#organization))
- The Code explicitly says not to expose a creator's legal identity when it is not explicitly noted in the zine. It recommends consulting creators and communities about autonomy and privacy. ([Code of Ethics, Access 2](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#access))
- QZL operationalizes this conservatively: if no name appears, leave the author blank; do not play detective; do not import a full name found online without permission. A donor-approved name may be used if the donation form authorizes it, with a note explaining the source. ([QZL manual, Author])
- QZL removes a name without question on request, records “Author's name has been removed by request of the creator,” checks other records for the same name, and warns against deadnaming or outing. If the creator gives a different permitted name, use the creator's preferred name and send the finished record for approval where possible. ([QZL manual, Removing a name])
- QZL distinguishes individual, collective, editor, author, and contributor roles. Collective names are recorded as collectives; compilation editors are not automatically treated as sole authors; additional contributors go in a separate field. ([QZL manual, Collective authors; Editors; Other authors])
- The Code encourages using creators' own terminology while acknowledging that cataloguers must balance it with collocation and discoverability. Subject terms that offend or do not resonate should be revisable through feedback from authors and users. ([Code of Ethics, Organization 3](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#organization))
- QZL's preferred description is creator-supplied text from a donation form or zine shop, quoted as the creator's words. Cataloguer-written summaries are a fallback, not a replacement for creator voice. ([QZL manual, Comments])

### Privacy, access, rights, and takedown

- The Code frames access as a conflict between equitable library access and creator privacy. When acquiring directly, tell the creator that the zine will be available to a broader public and, where possible, give a right of refusal for high visibility. It recognizes that pre-Internet creators may not have anticipated institutional or online exposure. ([Code of Ethics, Acquisition 2](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#acquisition); [Access 1](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#access))
- The Code's preferred acquisition order is direct purchase from the author/publisher, purchase through a distro, author/publisher donation, third-party donation, unauthorized copying, and unauthorized third-party purchase. The rationale is creator support, trust, and informed acquisition. ([Code of Ethics, Acquisition 1](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#acquisition))
- Public discoverability is not the same as permission to reproduce. The Code treats zines as copyrighted unless otherwise indicated, recommends permission for published images/substantial excerpts, and says a holding institution is not a proxy for the creator. It recommends honoring creator requests to remove or edit online descriptions or reproductions. ([Code of Ethics, Use](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#use))
- QZL places a takedown/correction notice in every public record and lets creators amend details or remove the zine. It seeks permission for cover images and removes or edits images on request. ([QZL manual, Comments; Cover image])
- Barnard permits researchers to scan or photograph for personal use, but says not to publish images or substantial text without every effort to obtain permission. Its access page also distinguishes open-stack zines from non-circulating special collections and directs researchers to the zine librarian when a requested item is unprocessed. ([Barnard, Collection Access & Circulation](https://zines.barnard.edu/collection-access-circulation))
- Separate discovery, reading, circulation, reproduction, and preservation decisions. Barnard's open stacks and special collections make this distinction operational; the Code says institutions should balance researcher access against creator wants and needs rather than assume one access rule fits all. ([Barnard access](https://zines.barnard.edu/collection-access-circulation); [Code of Ethics, Access](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#access))

### Metadata, description, and provenance

- Describe as fully as the institution can, but treat detail as a privacy risk: more detail increases discoverability while potentially exposing private information about living people. ([Code of Ethics, Organization 2](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#organization))
- Record the title as it appears on the zine. QZL uses a bracketed supplied title when no title exists, rather than leaving the title empty, and records the source whenever a title is taken from a distro, website, or email. ([QZL manual, Title; No title; Comments])
- Record uncertainty rather than silently converting inference into fact. QZL brackets externally sourced dates, marks estimates with a question mark, and uses an explicit “date ... not identified” statement when no estimate is possible. Place and publisher follow the same identified/not-identified convention. ([QZL manual, Publication date; Publication])
- Capture physical extent as physical evidence: page count (including counted unnumbered pages), number of volumes/copies, and height in centimeters. QZL intentionally leaves width, thickness, and weight blank when its workflow does not need them. Do not infer physical facts from a digital file. ([QZL manual, Physical description])
- Record language values, including a second or original language where a translation exists. QZL does not require ISBN because zines often do not have one. ([QZL manual, ISBN; Languages])
- Preserve meaningful extras and material context. The Code specifically flags inserts, stickers, pins, handwritten notes, and decorated envelopes as items requiring an explicit decision to retain with, separately from, or outside the zine. ([Code of Ethics, Preservation](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#preservation))
- QZL keeps public comments distinct from private comments. Public comments contain a disclaimer, description, notes, and free online access; private comments record operational restrictions such as damage or exhibition limits. ([QZL manual, Comments; Private comments])

### Controlled vocabularies and discoverability

- The Code recommends combining controlled and uncontrolled access. Use established thesauri for collocation, but add creator/community language, local headings, and keyword-rich summary notes when controlled vocabularies lack appropriate terms. Named options are LCSH, LCGFT, AAT, and the Anchor Archive Thesaurus. ([Code of Ethics, Organization 3-4](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/#organization))
- The zinelibraries.info cataloguing guide calls the Anchor Archive Subject Thesaurus a zine-specific controlled vocabulary and recommends AAT for physical attributes such as binding and printing styles, in addition to or instead of LCSH. ([Zine cataloging](https://www.zinelibraries.info/running-a-zine-library/zine-cataloging/))
- QZL limits each record to a maximum of five specific tags, prefers “punk” to “music,” “lesbian” to “LGBT,” and “painting” to “art,” and uses tags for what the zine is about, not merely that it is a zine. It checks existing tags first, discusses new tags at a cataloguing meeting, and consults Homosaurus, Anchor Archive, AAT, then LCSH. ([QZL manual, Tags])
- QZL explicitly asks cataloguers to examine colonial history, heteronormativity, white supremacy, transphobia, misogyny, and ableism when proposing terms. It keeps tags separate from person/place/organization authority headings, and uses searchable summaries for additional language. ([QZL manual, Tags])
- ZineCat exists because zine libraries use disparate schemas and controlled or uncontrolled vocabularies. Its stated goal is a shared, Linked Open Data catalog usable by large research institutions and small anarchist libraries. xZINECOREx is described as a Dublin Core-based basic metadata standard and starting point for shared cataloguing. ([ZineCat](https://www.zinelibraries.info/zine-union-catalog/))
- Barnard demonstrates institutional discoverability: records in CLIO include author, call number, subject headings, and a contents summary; inclusion in a large catalog and WorldCat enables serendipitous discovery and interlibrary loan. ([Barnard, About the Collection](https://zines.barnard.edu/about-collection); [Barnard, Access](https://zines.barnard.edu/collection-access-circulation))
- Barnard's local arrangement uses the fixed prefix `Zines` and a Cutter number, shelving by Cutter alone rather than pretending that a conventional subject class is the primary organizing principle. ([Barnard, Access](https://zines.barnard.edu/collection-access-circulation))

### Community review and collective decision-making

- The Code treats cataloguing as accountable community work, not neutral transcription. It recommends feedback paths for authors and users to request subject-record revisions and says zine libraries should be willing to consider creator requests about identification and treatment. ([Code of Ethics, Access and Organization](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/))
- QZL says cataloguing should not happen in a vacuum: volunteers should consult one another, other libraries, and zinesters. It plans for readers and makers to participate, and routes difficult cases to volunteer discussion. New local tags require collective discussion at a monthly cataloguing meeting. ([QZL manual, Introduction; Tags])
- QZL's manual is deliberately “living” and resource-aware: its local system and volunteer skills shape the procedure, and the procedure can change. This is a useful model for documenting decisions without claiming universal standards. ([QZL manual, Introduction])
- ZLuC is an ongoing peer infrastructure rather than a single authority. The event welcomes workers and volunteers from academic, public, special, independent, and community libraries/archives; archived pages preserve schedules, session notes, codes of conduct, and organizing documents. ([ZLuC archive](https://www.zinelibraries.info/events/zine-librarian-unconferences/); [ZLuC 2023](https://www.zinelibraries.info/wiki/zluc-2023-san-francisco/); [ZLuC 2024](https://www.zinelibraries.info/wiki/zluc-nyc-2024/))
- The ZLuC toolkit recommends a shared notes location, collaborative topic selection, explicit accessibility planning, and a code of conduct. Its recording/photo template makes sessions open by default only with consent safeguards: ask before audio/video recording, allow sessions to set stricter rules, and respect requests for no attribution or no photography. ([ZLuC Organizers Toolkit](https://www.zinelibraries.info/events/zine-librarian-unconferences/zluc-organizers-toolkit/))

### Collection policy and institutional/community context

- The Collection Policy Primer recommends publishing a written policy that states mission, audience/community, selection responsibility, formats, subjects, geography, languages, dates, diversity commitments, deaccession, and treatment of unsolicited gifts. It is both a selection tool and a transparent explanation of what the collection does not collect. ([Collection Policy Primer](https://www.zinelibraries.info/running-a-zine-library/collection-policy-primer/))
- The Primer's examples make scope identity- and content-based rather than reducible to creator identity: QZAP's cited policy collects by self-identified queer creators and queer material regardless of creator identity; Carnegie's cited policy balances subjects and community needs. Treat these as examples in the 2014 primer, not current policies verified independently here. ([Collection Policy Primer](https://www.zinelibraries.info/running-a-zine-library/collection-policy-primer/))
- Barnard's current collection description accepts zines by women, transfeminine people, trans women, and trans men, while welcoming any period or subject; its institutional purpose emphasizes current readers, scholars, future researchers, and the documentary value of voices otherwise lost. ([Barnard Zine Library](https://zines.barnard.edu/))
- ABC No Rio's current collection statement is explicitly place- and movement-aware: priority goes to Lower East Side/downtown Manhattan documentation, countercultures and DIY communities, zines about social relations, and practical peer advice. It identifies zines as records of movements often left out of official history and welcomes volunteers with or without library training. ([ABC No Rio Zine Library](https://exile.abcnorio.org/facilities/zine-library/))

## Practical implications for a zine catalogue

1. Store the form of creator name actually present on the zine, with authorship status and role kept separate from a reconciled identity. Never infer a legal identity from a web search.
2. Keep source and confidence with title, date, place, publisher, and summary values. Mark supplied, estimated, and not-identified values explicitly.
3. Use creator-supplied descriptions where available; retain cataloguer summaries as clearly identified descriptive work.
4. Combine a governed vocabulary with local/community terms and searchable free text. Record the vocabulary/source and make term changes reviewable.
5. Provide a visible correction/takedown route and treat creator requests to remove names, images, descriptions, or records as a normal workflow, not an exceptional dispute.
6. Model discovery, reading, download, preservation, lending, reproduction, and reuse as separate decisions. A public record or public file is not blanket permission.
7. Record physical extent and material features from the physical object, including unnumbered pages and meaningful inserts; do not collapse digital page count into physical extent.
8. Publish collection scope and selection/deaccession rules, and state who decides. Include local geography, language, community, and diversity commitments where they are part of the mission.
9. Build review into cataloguing: consult peers and creators, provide author/user feedback channels, and use collective meetings for new or contested terms.
10. Preserve decision provenance and policy versions. The Code is explicitly a living, non-prescriptive document, and QZL's manual is explicitly a living volunteer procedure.

## Limitations and source-quality notes

- The Code of Ethics is a 2015 collaborative snapshot still hosted by zinelibraries.info; the site says in 2022 and again in 2026 that a revised edition is in progress. Treat it as influential practitioner guidance, not a current formal standard.
- QZL's manual is highly actionable but local: it reflects LibraryThing/TinyCat fields, volunteer capacity, and QZL's own ethics. Some field choices should not be copied without checking the target catalogue's needs.
- The ZLuC archive preserves community process and operational practice, but not every session has publicly accessible notes. Absence of a posted session note is not evidence that a topic was not discussed.
- ABC No Rio's present website is in a period of construction/exile; its collection mission and history are still useful first-party evidence, but current access hours should be checked before operational use.
- The zinelibraries.info cataloguing page lists practitioner-authored academic work, including Jenna Freedman and Rhonda Kauffman's *Cutter and Paste* (2013), Heidy Berthoud's “My Life as a ‘Like-Minded Misfit’” (2018), and Allison Jai O'Dell's “RDA and the Description of Zines” (2014). The page verifies their bibliographic identity and relevance, but this note does not infer detailed guidance from those works without reviewing their full text.
