# Books on zines, DIY publishing, and zine archives

Research completed 2026-09-24. This is a selective bibliography of book-length
works that help with one or more of the following: zine form, making and
circulation, communities, authorship, political use, collecting, description,
and access. Bibliographic facts are recorded from the linked publisher or
library/archive record, not inferred from a search-result snippet.

## How to read the evidence notes

Internet Archive records are useful library surrogates: they expose title,
creator, date, publisher, ISBN, extent, subjects, contents notes, and often a
MARCXML record. They do not necessarily provide open reading access. A record
marked `Access-restricted-item` is evidence that the catalogue metadata is
available, not evidence that the book can be freely downloaded or fully read.

## Strongest matches for Biblioteca

### Kate Eichhorn, *The Archival Turn in Feminism: Outrage in Order*

- **Author:** Kate Eichhorn
- **Year:** 2013
- **Publisher:** Temple University Press, Philadelphia
- **Type:** Scholarly monograph; archival and feminist cultural history
- **Bibliographic evidence:** [Internet Archive library record](https://archive.org/details/archivalturninfe0000eich)
  gives the title, author, 2013 date, publisher, ISBNs `9781439909515`,
  `1439909512`, and extent `xii, 188 pages`. Its supplied description says
  that the book examines feminist cultural artefacts and their collection,
  cataloging, preservation, and distribution, including the Sallie Bingham
  Center, the Riot Grrrl Collection at NYU, and the Barnard Zine Library. The
  record also supplies the contents list and a [MARCXML
  record](https://archive.org/download/archivalturninfe0000eich/archivalturninfe0000eich_archive_marc.xml).
- **Key contribution:** The most directly useful work here for institutional
  practice. It connects riot grrrl and feminist zines to the transition from
  activist/circulating artefacts to collected archival objects, and treats
  archivists, cataloguers, preservation, and distribution as part of the
  movement's afterlife rather than as neutral technical operations.
- **Biblioteca implications:** Keep publication description distinct from
  holding and archive-steward context. Record collection/series relationships
  and the institution or collection through which a zine is known. Preserve
  provenance for cataloguer-supplied descriptions, and distinguish discovery,
  reading, downloading, preservation, and reuse permissions. Do not let an
  institutional collection name stand in for creator, publisher, or rights
  holder. This work also supports recording later archival custody as a
  contextual role assertion rather than rewriting the original publication's
  authorship.
- **Access limitation:** Internet Archive exposes rich metadata and a
  restricted bookreader item, but the page says `Access-restricted-item` and
  offers no suitable unrestricted download. The contribution summary above is
  grounded in the record's publisher description and contents note; full
  argument-level use requires library access or the publisher's copy.

### Alison Piepmeier, *Girl Zines: Making Media, Doing Feminism*

- **Author:** Alison Piepmeier
- **Year:** 2009
- **Publisher:** New York University Press, New York
- **Type:** Scholarly monograph; feminist media and cultural analysis
- **Bibliographic evidence:** [Internet Archive library record](https://archive.org/details/girlzinesmakingm0000piep)
  gives the title, author, 2009 date, publisher, ISBNs `9780814767511`,
  `0814767516`, `9780814767528`, and `0814767524`, extent `xiv, 249 p.`, and
  the contents. The contents include “Why zines matter: materiality and the
  creation of embodied community,” intersectional identities, zines as public
  pedagogy, and an appendix, “Where to find zines.” The record supplies a
  [MARCXML record](https://archive.org/download/girlzinesmakingm0000piep/girlzinesmakingm0000piep_archive_marc.xml).
- **Key contribution:** A focused account of girl and grrrl zines as media
  practices: material form helps make embodied communities; zines can be
  feminist pedagogy and a site for negotiating non-unified or intersectional
  identities. The chapter structure makes the scope and method unusually easy
  to verify without pretending that the work is a general history of every
  zine tradition.
- **Biblioteca implications:** Treat materiality and community as descriptive
  evidence, not as decorative subject tags. A record should be able to capture
  zine form, reproduction method, physical extent, and assembly separately,
  while also recording communities, movements, and self-described identities
  as contextual subjects when the publication supports them. Preserve the
  publication's own language and the source of any identity or community
  description. “Where to find zines” also points toward circulation/discovery
  provenance: a source, distributor, or collection is not automatically the
  creator or rights-holder.
- **Access limitation:** The Internet Archive scan is marked
  `Access-restricted-item`; its metadata and contents are accessible, while
  the full scan is not an unrestricted download. Some text is noted as running
  into the gutter, which is also a reminder that a digital surrogate can lose
  or distort material-form evidence.

### Stephen Duncombe, *Notes from Underground: Zines and the Politics of Alternative Culture*

- **Author:** Stephen Duncombe
- **Year:** 1997 (the consulted record is a reprint/scan)
- **Publisher:** Verso, London and New York
- **Type:** Scholarly/critical cultural sociology and political analysis
- **Bibliographic evidence:** [Internet Archive library record](https://archive.org/details/notesfromundergr00dunc)
  gives the 1997 publication date, publisher, ISBNs `1859848273`,
  `9781859848272`, `1859841589`, and `9781859841587`, extent 262 pages, and
  “Includes bibliographical references (p. 199-230) and index.” Its subject
  headings are “Zines,” “Press and politics,” and “Alternativpresse.” The
  record identifies the scanned copy as a reprint and links a [MARCXML
  record](https://archive.org/download/notesfromundergr00dunc/notesfromundergr00dunc_archive_marc.xml).
- **Key contribution:** A foundational political-cultural framing of zines as
  alternative press and as a form of participation, rather than merely as
  small magazines. It is especially useful for asking what zine production,
  readership, and anti-mainstream circulation do politically. The available
  record verifies that framing through the title and subjects; it does not
  expose enough of the text to make chapter-level claims.
- **Biblioteca implications:** Model distribution and audience as meaningful
  publication context: exchange, network, event, scene, or circulation route
  should not be collapsed into publisher or place of publication. Record
  claims about politics and community with source and scope, avoiding a generic
  “alternative” label as an automatic classification. Keep original date,
  edition/reprint date, and the date of the consulted surrogate distinct.
- **Access limitation:** Internet Archive exposes catalogue metadata and a
  restricted item with bibliographic references and index information, but the
  page says `Access-restricted-item` and provides no suitable unrestricted
  download. The book is therefore a high-priority acquisition or library-loan
  target for close reading, not a source from which to quote unseen passages.

### Amy Spencer, *DIY: The Rise of Lo-Fi Culture*

- **Author:** Amy Spencer (the library record gives “Amy Spencer, 1979-”)
- **Year:** 2005
- **Publisher:** Marion Boyars, London and New York
- **Type:** Cultural history/critical account of DIY practice; not presented as
  a peer-reviewed zine-only monograph
- **Bibliographic evidence:** [Internet Archive library record](https://archive.org/details/diyriseofloficul0000spen)
  gives the title, author, date, publisher, ISBNs `9780714531052` and
  `0714531057`, and a 390-page scan record. Its subject headings include
  “Fan magazines,” “Self-publishing,” “Sound recordings -- Production and
  direction,” and “Radio and music.” It also says the book includes
  bibliographical references and links a [MARCXML
  record](https://archive.org/download/diyriseofloficul0000spen/diyriseofloficul0000spen_archive_marc.xml).
- **Key contribution:** Useful as a broader DIY ecology rather than as a
  zine theory text. The catalogue subjects place fan magazines and
  self-publishing alongside music and radio production, making it relevant to
  cross-media networks, low-cost making, and the fact that publication
  communities often include sound, performance, and distribution practices.
  The accessible record does not provide a detailed chapter list or synopsis,
  so narrower claims should wait for the book itself.
- **Biblioteca implications:** Do not assume “zine” exhausts the publication's
  form or network. Separate zine format from reproduction method and from
  related media. Record collaborators, collectives, labels, venues, and
  distributors as role assertions when evidence supports them; do not infer
  authorship from a publisher or scene affiliation. A free-text description
  can preserve DIY language while controlled subjects remain governed terms.
- **Access limitation:** The Internet Archive item is marked
  `Access-restricted-item`; metadata is available, but no unrestricted full
  text was exposed. The contribution is therefore a carefully bounded
  catalogue-level assessment, not a substitute for reading the work.

### Teal Triggs, *Fanzines: The DIY Revolution*

- **Author:** Teal Triggs
- **Year:** 2010
- **Publisher:** Chronicle Books, San Francisco; GBS listed as distributor in
  the consulted record
- **Type:** Visual history/reference work with critical framing; not a
  conventional scholarly monograph
- **Bibliographic evidence:** [Internet Archive library record](https://archive.org/details/fanzinesdiyrevol0000trig)
  gives the title, author, 2010 date, publisher/distributor, ISBN `9780811876926`,
  extent `256 p. : 34 cm`, and subjects “Fan magazines -- History -- Pictorial
  works,” “Graphic arts -- History -- Pictorial works,” and “Counterculture --
  History -- Pictorial works.” It includes bibliographical references (pp.
  248-254) and an index, and links a [MARCXML
  record](https://archive.org/download/fanzinesdiyrevol0000trig/fanzinesdiyrevol0000trig_archive_marc.xml).
- **Key contribution:** Its large visual format and pictorial-history scope
  make it useful for studying zine design, page composition, graphic style,
  and the relation between visual evidence and historical narration. It is
  also a reminder that a zine can be catalogued as a visual/graphic object, not
  only as a text-bearing serial.
- **Biblioteca implications:** Capture format and visual/material features as
  first-class metadata, but do not infer reproduction method from an image or a
  digital PDF. Physical dimensions, page count, binding/assembly, image-heavy
  form, and scans with missing or reordered pages need separate evidence. A
  digital surrogate should retain links to the source and note whether its
  page sequence and colour reproduce the physical publication.
- **Access limitation:** Internet Archive provides metadata and a restricted
  bookreader record; it says `Access-restricted-item` and “No suitable files to
  display here.” The full visual argument and image sequence should be checked
  in a physical or licensed copy.

### Roger Sabin and Teal Triggs, *Below Critical Radar: Fanzines and Alternative Comics from 1976 to Now*

- **Authors:** Roger Sabin and Teal Triggs
- **Year:** 2002 in the consulted library record (some secondary listings may
  show other dates; use the holding's date and ISBN when citing a copy)
- **Publisher:** Slab-O-Concrete, Hove, United Kingdom
- **Type:** Co-authored critical/visual history; adjacent to zines through
  fanzines and alternative comics
- **Bibliographic evidence:** [Internet Archive library record](https://archive.org/details/belowcriticalrad00sabi)
  gives the title, both authors, 2002 date, publisher, ISBNs `1899866477` and
  `9781899866472`, extent 124 pages, and bibliographical references. It links
  a [MARCXML record](https://archive.org/download/belowcriticalrad00sabi/belowcriticalrad00sabi_archive_marc.xml).
  The same record explicitly warns: “No title page found. No copyright page
  found.”
- **Key contribution:** A bridge between fanzine history and alternative-comics
  history, useful for understanding how scenes, genres, and publication forms
  overlap without being identical. It encourages treating “fanzine,” “zine,”
  and “alternative comics” as historically situated terms rather than silently
  interchangeable categories.
- **Biblioteca implications:** Use a publication's self-description and
  evidence to record genre/form relationships, rather than assigning a single
  universal type. Allow related publications and scenes to be linked without
  flattening them into one series. The missing title/copyright pages are a
  concrete case for provenance and uncertainty: record the source of author,
  date, and publisher claims and preserve unresolved bibliographic questions.
- **Access limitation:** The Internet Archive copy is access-restricted and
  lacks title and copyright pages. Its bibliographic facts are consequently
  less secure than a publisher or title-page record and should be checked
  against another library copy before being used as authoritative data.

### V. Vale, *Zines! Volume One*

- **Author/editorial agent:** V. Vale (the record also indexes the creator as
  “Vale, V”); treat the role as editor/interviewer or compiler pending title
  page confirmation, not automatically as sole author of every contribution
- **Year:** 1996
- **Publisher:** The consulted record gives `[San Francisco?, CA : V. Vale]`,
  with the place and publisher explicitly bracketed/uncertain
- **Type:** Primary-source interview/reference compilation and activist/scene
  history, rather than scholarly analysis
- **Bibliographic evidence:** [Internet Archive library record](https://archive.org/details/zinesvolumeone00vval)
  gives the 1996 date, title, ISBN `0965046907` (and a second ISBN
  `0965046923`), LCCN `96090163`, 190 pages, subjects including zines,
  underground press, publishing, editors, and interviews. It notes that
  volumes 1-2 include a “Directory of zines” and indexes, and links a
  [MARCXML record](https://archive.org/download/zinesvolumeone00vval/zinesvolumeone00vval_archive_marc.xml).
- **Key contribution:** A scene-adjacent primary compilation: interviews and a
  directory preserve participants' own accounts and names of publications.
  That makes it valuable for authorship, self-identification, networks, and
  historical discovery, while its editorial/compiled form must not be confused
  with a neutral scholarly survey.
- **Biblioteca implications:** Model editor/interviewer/compiler and
  interviewed or featured agents separately. A directory entry can be a lead
  or discovery source, not proof that a title, date, creator, address, or
  circulation claim is current. Preserve the exact source and uncertainty of
  directory-derived metadata, and support aliases, pseudonyms, collectives,
  and unresolved authorship. Record the volume/series relationship rather than
  treating “Zines!” as one undifferentiated work.
- **Access limitation:** The record is access-restricted, and its publisher and
  place are presented with brackets/question mark. It exposes catalogue
  metadata and notes about the directory/indexes but not a verified open full
  text. The volume's internal contributor roles should be transcribed from a
  title/copyright page or inspected copy before cataloguing them.

## Cross-source metadata conclusions

1. **Form is not subject.** Triggs, Piepmeier, and the archive records support
   separate treatment of visual/material form, genre, subject, and political or
   community context.
2. **A zine has more than one relevant agent.** Piepmeier and Eichhorn make
   community and institutional mediation visible; Vale makes editor,
   interviewer, contributor, and directory roles especially important. Keep
   creator, publisher, distributor, submitter, collection, and archive steward
   as contextual role assertions.
3. **Circulation is evidence, not identity.** Duncombe, Spencer, and Vale point
   toward exchange networks, scenes, and self-publishing practices. Record
   sources and circulation claims without turning a venue, distributor, or
   archive into the author.
4. **Archival description changes the object.** Eichhorn is the clearest warning
   that collecting, cataloging, preservation, and distribution are part of a
   zine's later history. Keep the publication description, holding, file asset,
   access policy, and provenance claim distinct.
5. **Absence and uncertainty should survive cataloguing.** Sabin/Triggs lacks a
   title and copyright page; Vale's publisher/place is uncertain; restricted
   scans expose metadata without full inspection. A catalogue should preserve
   these evidence conditions rather than silently filling gaps.
6. **Access is multidimensional.** A library record, a restricted preview, a
   public reading copy, and permission to download or reuse are different
   states. These books provide a useful analogue for recording source,
   surrogate, access limitation, and evidence quality separately.
