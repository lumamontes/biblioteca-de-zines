# Brazilian zine collections, archives, and scholarship

Research date: 2026-09-24

## Scope and method

This note reviews Brazil-focused or Brazilian sources that expose either public
catalogue metadata, a collection/archival interface, a first-party project
description, or scholarship/repository metadata about zines and fanzines. The
priority was evidence of what is actually recorded, rather than a general
definition of zines.

Sources were checked as first-party pages where possible: a project or publisher
site, a university/repository record, a journal landing page, or a direct DOI
record. Search-engine result pages were used only to discover candidate sources,
not as evidence. All examples in this note are synthetic. No private contact
details or private contributor data are reproduced.

This is a comparative research note, not a claim that any source is a Brazilian
cataloguing standard. The public Brazilian evidence is heterogeneous: some
sources are structured catalogue records, some are community archives, and some
are scholarly descriptions of zine production rather than library policies.

## Executive findings

1. The clearest Brazil-focused public catalogue found is Biblioteca de Zines. Its
   public records consistently expose title, creator or collective, year,
   categories, a short description, a cover/preview, and a reader-facing zine
   link or embedded delivery path. Some records preserve place and community
   context in description text, but place, UF, municipality, language, physical
   extent, reproduction method, rights, and formal holdings are not consistently
   exposed as dedicated public fields.
2. Zine Gororoba, linked to the UTFPR Curitiba art-zine initiative, demonstrates
   a different archival pattern: a community/student collection organised by
   edition and title, with links to Google Drive or FlipHTML5 delivery copies,
   event context, series/collection labels, and occasional dates. It is rich in
   production and community context but does not present a stable library-style
   record schema.
3. Marca de Fantasia is a long-running independent Brazilian comics/zine
   publisher and digital catalogue. Its pages expose title, author/editor,
   series or section, cover image, publication grouping, and in some cases a
   persistent page path. It is strong evidence for creator and series context,
   especially from Paraiba, but it is a publisher catalogue rather than a
   neutral library holding record.
4. Brazilian scholarship treats fanzines as material, social, educational, and
   discursive publications. The Brazilian articles and thesis expose title,
   creator, institutional affiliation, date, language, abstract, keywords or
   topical framing, DOI/repository identifier, and sometimes page extent. They
   generally do not provide a zine-object cataloguing policy or a controlled
   vocabulary for reproduction, physical extent, or rights.
5. Across these sources, place is most useful when retained as context with a
   role: publication/creator location, event location, circulation community,
   or institutional affiliation. A collection's location should not be copied
   into every zine record as the zine's place of publication.
6. Rights and access are the largest evidence gap. Public preview/download
   links show delivery or discovery, not necessarily copyright permission,
   digitisation consent, or permission to make derivatives.

## Source 1: Biblioteca de Zines

### Collection and policy evidence

The About page describes the project as a free, open-source initiative intended to
provide a space for independent zines from Brazil. It says the collection includes
zines with different themes made by people from different places in the country.
It defines zines as independent publications made by one or more people and names
collage, drawing, text, and photography as possible forms. These are collection
scope and project-description statements, not facts that should be assigned to
every individual record.

The same page links to related Brazilian projects, including a UTFPR Curitiba art
zine initiative, a collaborative digital zineteca, and Marca de Fantasia. Those
links are evidence of a local ecosystem of independent and educational projects;
they do not prove that Biblioteca de Zines holds the linked collections.

**Evidence source:** [About - Biblioteca de Zines](https://www.biblioteca-de-zines.com.br/about).

### Public catalogue fields actually visible

The browse page exposes a search box labelled for zine or author, a year filter,
and category filtering. Each result visibly repeats a compact pattern:

- title;
- creator name, collective, or multiple creators;
- year;
- one or more category labels;
- cover image or fallback image;
- a short description when supplied; and
- a link to a detail page.

The detail page adds a title/creator heading, creator profile links when present,
repeatable category labels, and a longer public description. The public record
may include a collection/series statement and a place or community reference in
the description, but the interface does not expose these as dedicated structured
place fields.

**Evidence sources:** [browse](https://www.biblioteca-de-zines.com.br/zines),
[detail page example](https://www.biblioteca-de-zines.com.br/zines/coletivo-matinta-matinta-zine-05),
and [a second detail page](https://www.biblioteca-de-zines.com.br/zines/ali-el-kadre-mes-internacional-de-zines).

### What the public records do and do not record

| Requested area | Evidence in public interface | Evidence gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Format/form | Category labels include forms or modes such as digital art, collage, comics, photography, and experimental; these are mixed with topical or programme labels | No stable distinction between genre/form, subject, and delivery format is exposed |
| Physical extent | Not visible in the checked browse/detail pages | No page count, dimensions, binding, inserts, or copy-specific extent |
| Reproduction | Descriptions may mention production context, but no dedicated reproduction field was visible | No controlled value for photocopy, risograph, offset, hand-produced, or unknown |
| Place/UF/municipality | Some descriptions identify communities or cities, such as artists from Santarem and Belem, Para | Place role, municipality authority, UF, and evidence source are not separate fields |
| Language | Not visible as a dedicated field | Do not infer Portuguese from the site or title |
| Subjects | Category labels and description text provide topical discovery | No visible vocabulary/version, subject-vs-form distinction, or creator-supplied label provenance |
| Creators/collectives | Public heading, creator links, and collective names; multiple contributors can be shown | Role, authorship status, pseudonym/anonymity, ordering, and consent are not visible as separate assertions |
| Rights | The project is openly readable and some linked projects are described as free to download | No per-zine rights statement or permission scope is visible; public availability is not legal permission |
| Community context | Description text can preserve a series, collective, workshop, or local scene | Context is unstructured prose and may be difficult to query or preserve independently |
| Holding/file evidence | Cover and reader links are visible | No repository holding, physical copy, source/provenance, checksum, derivative, or custody assertion |

### Intake evidence

The submission form asks for one or more authors, optional social links, one or
more zines, and an optional contact field. This is important evidence about the
project's intake boundary: a creator or collective is a first-class submission
subject, and multiple zines may be submitted together. It is not evidence that
the corresponding values are all public catalogue fields, nor that the submitter
is the rights-holder.

**Evidence source:** [Sugerir zine](https://www.biblioteca-de-zines.com.br/zines/apply).

### Metadata assessment

Biblioteca de Zines is the strongest directly inspectable Brazilian example of a
reader-facing zine catalogue in this review. Its strengths are discoverability,
creator/collective display, multiple categories, short contextual description,
and links from a publication to an agent page. Its public shape is intentionally
lightweight. It should not be read as evidence that a zine has no physical form,
that a category is a controlled subject, or that a hosted reading link establishes
rights or preservation custody.

## Source 2: Zine Gororoba and the UTFPR-linked zineteca

The first-party Linktree identifies `Zine de Arte da UTFPR Curitiba` and presents
itself as a repository of links and drives. It includes a `ZINETECA` link and a
separate section for digital zines. The digital section lists named publications
and links to Google Drive or FlipHTML5 copies. The page also exposes edition labels
such as `Edicao #0`, `Edicao #1`, `Edicao #2 - Gourmet`, and `Edicao #3 -
Comestivel`, as well as flash/event zines.

The descriptive text around individual links records context that a conventional
catalogue often loses:

- an initiative or collection name, such as a series or `Colezine`;
- an event or workshop occasion;
- a city or local cultural reference, including Curitiba in an event title or
  description;
- a production date for at least some event zines;
- a theme or production prompt; and
- the delivery location of a digital reading copy.

This is evidence of a collection organised through links, series/edition labels,
and community events. It is not evidence of a stable record for every zine. The
page does not expose page count, dimensions, reproduction method, language,
rights, creator role, or a formal holding/custody statement for each link.

**Evidence source:** [Zine Gororoba first-party link hub](https://linktr.ee/zinegororoba).

### Metadata assessment

Zine Gororoba shows why `series`, `issue/edition`, `event`, `place`, and `community
context` should not be flattened into one description. A zine can be both an
edition in a recurring project and the outcome of a workshop or event. The link
hub also demonstrates a practical risk: a public delivery URL can be the only
stable-looking evidence available while source bytes, version history, rights,
and custody remain unknown.

The UTFPR connection is recorded here as the project page states it. The source
does not establish that the university has adopted a library policy for these
zines, so it must not be cited as an institutional cataloguing rule.

## Source 3: Marca de Fantasia

Marca de Fantasia is a Brazilian independent publishing site whose first-party
home page exposes sections including independent comics, magazines, and related
projects. The page presents publication covers, titles, named creators/editors,
series or section paths, and persistent HTML links. It identifies the publisher
as operating from Joao Pessoa, Paraiba, but that address is publisher context and
not automatically the place of publication for every listed work.

The site is useful as an institutional/independent publishing archive because it
preserves relationships that a one-record zine form might miss:

- title to creator/editor;
- title to series or recurring publication section;
- title to cover/preview image;
- title to a stable page path; and
- title to a publisher-level collection context.

The site is not a library catalogue. A publisher page may include promotional
language, publication groupings, or a reading/download link without recording a
physical holding, number of copies, condition, shelfmark, or rights scope.

**Evidence source:** [Marca de Fantasia](https://marcadefantasia.com/).

### Metadata assessment

The relevant lesson is not to copy the publisher's model wholesale. It is to
retain publisher/press as a contextual role, preserve series membership, and
distinguish publisher location from the zine's creation, publication, or
circulation place. A local catalogue should retain the publisher's own title and
series wording while adding any normalized access point separately.

## Source 4: Brazilian repository and scholarship evidence

### Universidade de Brasilia thesis on alternative-publication cataloguing

The UnB repository record identifies Felipe de Jesus Rodrigues da Silva's 2019
undergraduate thesis, *Catalogacao de publicacoes alternativas: um estudo sobre o
fanzine e o cordel*. The title itself is direct evidence that Brazilian library
scholarship has treated fanzines and cordel as related alternative-publication
cataloguing problems. The repository record supplies a formal scholarly object
with title, author, institution, year, and persistent handle.

The repository record is not enough to claim the thesis's detailed field-level
recommendations. The full text should be read before using it as a policy source.
This note therefore uses it as evidence of Brazilian scholarship and terminology,
not as proof of any specific required field.

**Evidence source:** [BDM/UnB repository record](https://bdm.unb.br/handle/10483/22374).

### Brazilian journal article: Zavam, 2006

The DOI record identifies Aurea Suely Zavam's article `FANZINE: A PLURIVALENCIA
PARATOPICA` in *Linguagem em (Dis)curso*, volume 6, issue 1, pages 9-28, published
in 2006. The record includes the Portuguese title, an English alternative title,
author, journal, issue, pages, date, DOI, and a subject framing around literary
fanzines and discourse.

This is useful evidence that a Brazilian scholarly record can preserve alternate
title language and a precise journal extent. It is not evidence of a zine-level
physical extent or of a library subject vocabulary. The article's descriptive
focus is discursive and literary, so a catalogue should not treat its topical
framing as a universal classification of fanzines.

**Evidence source:** [SciELO/DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1590/1982-4017-06-01-01).

### Brazilian journal article: Zauith, Davanco and Barbieri, 2019

The USP journal DOI record identifies `Oficina de fanzine` in *Comunicacao &
Educacao*, volume 24, issue 1, pages 56-68, published in 2019. Its abstract says
that the Casa da Ciencia workshop operated during 2017 and 2018, produced more
than 100 zines, and involved students as authors of texts and illustrations. The
record therefore exposes:

- work title;
- three named authors;
- journal, volume, issue, and page extent;
- publication date and DOI; and
- a community/educational production context in the abstract.

The abstract distinguishes the workshop's authorship and learning context from
the article authorship. That distinction is important: the people who write
about a zine-making project are not necessarily the creators of every zine made
in it. A catalogue should model those roles separately.

**Evidence source:** [USP journal DOI record](https://doi.org/10.11606/issn.2316-9125.v24i1p56-68).

### Brazilian journal article: Lima, Salvador and Pereira, 2019

The UFSM DOI record identifies `Fanzine - arquitetura e revolucao` in *Ciencia e
Natura*, pages 168-182, published in 2019. Its abstract describes a fanzine made
as an exercise in an architecture and urbanism course, gives the title, explains
the historical/architectural topics, and describes visual references including
political posters.

This source is evidence for recording educational production context, topic, and
visual/reproduction context in descriptive notes. It does not expose a structured
zine record with page count, dimensions, printing process, or rights. The article
metadata itself records journal page extent, which must not be confused with the
physical extent of the fanzine discussed.

**Evidence source:** [UFSM journal DOI record](https://doi.org/10.5902/2179460x35516).

### Strongest cross-source evidence

- **Creators and collectives:** Biblioteca de Zines and Marca de Fantasia show
  public creator/collective attribution as a primary discovery feature. The USP
  workshop article shows why article authors and zine makers need separate roles.
- **Format versus subject:** Biblioteca de Zines demonstrates category-driven
  discovery, but its public categories can mix form, theme, and programme. The
  Zine Gororoba links show that an edition or event label is not the same thing
  as a subject heading.
- **Series and issue context:** Zine Gororoba exposes edition labels most
  directly. Marca de Fantasia exposes publisher/series groupings. Biblioteca de
  Zines records series context in prose for at least some records. These support
  repeatable series/issue relationships rather than copying a series name into a
  generic subject field.
- **Place:** The Biblioteca de Zines public description and Zine Gororoba event
  material preserve local scenes; Marca de Fantasia exposes a publisher location;
  scholarly articles expose institution and workshop context. None of these
  sources supports treating one place value as the answer to creation,
  publication, circulation, or holding location.
- **Physical extent and reproduction:** The public Brazilian sources are weak on
  dedicated zine-object fields. Scholarly records expose article page extent,
  but that is not zine page extent. Production and visual context appears mainly
  in prose or project descriptions.
- **Rights:** The public sources mainly demonstrate access or distribution links.
  They do not provide evidence for a complete per-zine legal, digitisation,
  derivative, preservation, or replication permission model.

## Evidence versus recommendation

The following recommendations are derived from the comparison and from the
repository's existing domain vocabulary. They are not claims that any one
Brazilian source already implements them.

### Recommended publication-level fields

- `title_as_presented`, plus an optional normalized search title;
- repeatable agent assertions with role, order, display name, authorship status,
  and source;
- `series` and `issue_designation` as relationships/statements, not only prose;
- distinct `form/genre` and `subject` assertions, each with vocabulary/source;
- creator-supplied description preserved separately from cataloguer summary;
- language of publication/content, without inferring it from the title or website;
- source/display date plus normalized date or interval, precision, and date role;
- place assertion with role such as creation, publication, printing, circulation,
  event, or creator context;
- optional Brazilian state/UF and municipality only when the source supports them;
- reproduction method as an observation or supplied statement, not an inference;
- physical extent with page/sheet count, dimensions, binding/assembly, and notes;
- a separate note for materially meaningful features such as inserts, folds,
  handwritten pagination, collage, or loose sheets.

### Recommended holding and asset fields

- repository or steward, distinct from creator/publisher;
- physical holding/copy, location or shelfmark, condition, and custody evidence;
- digital asset kind, source URL, file format, checksum, and derivative relation;
- discovery, reading, download, preservation, replication, and reuse decisions as
  separate access dimensions;
- source and provenance for a scan or reading copy;
- an unresolved/ambiguous match state when a local file resembles a catalogue
  publication but identity is not established.

### Recommended rights and community fields

- original rights/copying statement preserved verbatim with source;
- separate legal-rights claim, creator copying request, repository permission,
  and operational access decision;
- scope of each decision: publication, issue, physical copy, file, page, or
  derivative;
- field- and relationship-level visibility rather than one public/private switch;
- creator-supplied wording retained beside any normalized subject or form term;
- community context with an explicit visibility decision when a neighborhood,
  venue, event, or collective relationship could be sensitive;
- correction, suppression, withdrawal, and consent history without requiring a
  pseudonymous creator to reveal an offline identity.

## Synthetic examples for applying the evidence

These examples are invented and are not copied from any cited source.

### Synthetic record A: collective and place

`Paper Corridor`, credited to `Risco Coletivo`, is described as a monthly zine
made by artists in a fictional municipality in Para. Record `Risco Coletivo` as a
collective agent; record the municipality and UF as a place-of-creation claim only
if the source says so; record `monthly issue` as series/issue context; and do not
infer that the Biblioteca holding location is the publication place.

### Synthetic record B: workshop production

`Lines of Air` is a zine made during a fictional university workshop. Record the
workshop as community/production context, the participants as creator assertions
when the zine or consented project source supports that role, and the article or
teacher authors separately as documentation or editorial roles. Do not turn the
journal article's page count into the zine's physical extent.

### Synthetic record C: digital delivery and rights uncertainty

`Night Bus Pamphlet` is linked from a public drive. Record the URL as a discovered
delivery asset with unknown custody and unknown reuse permission unless stronger
evidence exists. If the cover says `copy freely`, preserve that statement as a
creator copying request, but do not treat it as automatic permission to digitize,
host, or create derivatives.

### Synthetic record D: material form

`Folded Weather` is observed as eight folded sheets with one loose insert and
photocopy marks. Preserve the observation, reproduction method, and assembly
features. Do not normalize it to a conventional page count without retaining the
source observation, and do not infer these facts from a PDF preview.

## Source limitations and unresolved research questions

- Brazilian zine cataloguing evidence is much more visible in project pages,
  independent publishing sites, and scholarly descriptions than in publicly
  documented library policies.
- The UnB thesis is a high-value Brazilian cataloguing lead, but its detailed
  recommendations need verification against the full text before use as a field
  authority.
- The public Biblioteca de Zines interface is useful for actual record observation,
  but its page view does not expose a formal schema, revision history, or complete
  preservation policy.
- Zine Gororoba's UTFPR connection is asserted by its own first-party link hub;
  this note does not infer university ownership or institutional endorsement.
- Marca de Fantasia is an important Brazilian publisher/archive example, but its
  descriptive choices are shaped by publication and distribution rather than
  library holdings management.
- Journal and repository metadata describe scholarly works, workshop projects, or
  theses. They should not be mistaken for item-level metadata for every zine
  discussed in those works.
- A later phase should inspect the full UnB thesis, search Brazilian university
  library catalogues for item-level `fanzine` records, and look for publicly
  versioned collection policies from municipal/state libraries or archives.
- No source reviewed establishes that public hosting, a public drive, a free
  download, or an anti-copyright phrase alone proves permission for all forms of
  reuse.
