# Zine Scholarship and Professional Practice

## Final report for Biblioteca

Research reviewed: 24 September 2026

This report answers what the reviewed academic books, peer-reviewed and academic
research, and experienced zine librarians and cataloguers say about describing
zines. It gives particular attention to Biblioteca and Brazilian zines. The
report distinguishes evidence from recommendation: a source's argument is not
automatically a field specification, and a field visible in one catalogue is not
automatically a universal practice.

Examples in this report are synthetic. They do not reproduce contributor names,
private production data, or source-record examples.

## Executive conclusion

The strongest conclusion is not that zines require an exotic alternative to
library metadata. It is that ordinary bibliographic description is insufficient
when used alone. Zines make several usually hidden distinctions operational:

- publication, issue, edition, series, physical copy, digital asset, and
  derivative are different objects;
- creator, collective, editor, illustrator, publisher, distributor, submitter,
  workshop participant, and archive steward are different roles;
- form/genre, subject, delivery format, reproduction method, and material
  features are different claims;
- a creator's public name is not necessarily a legal identity;
- a place of creation is not a publisher location, archive location, or country
  discovery tag;
- discovery, reading, downloading, preservation, replication, and reuse are
  different permissions or operational decisions.

Academic work explains why these distinctions matter: zines derive meaning from
material form, production, circulation, social position, and community
relationships. Practitioner work turns that insight into cautious procedures:
use the name on the piece, do not investigate anonymous creators, preserve
creator language, record uncertainty, consult communities, and provide
correction and takedown routes. Brazilian evidence supports this approach but
does not establish a national metadata standard.

For Biblioteca, the practical answer is a small required public core surrounded
by source-qualified, repeatable assertions and separate holding/asset/access
layers. ZineCore2 is a useful interoperability reference because it already
separates zines, agents, holdings, and repositories. Professional practice goes
further in creator autonomy, community review, field-level visibility,
uncertainty, ethical provenance, and the difference between a public record and
permission to reproduce a zine.

### 1.3 Practitioner guidance and operational examples

These sources are experienced professional or community practice, not all
formal standards:

- The collaborative [Zine Librarians Code of Ethics](https://www.zinelibraries.info/code-of-ethics-1115-web-version/) was issued by the Zine Librarians Interest Group in 2015; the web page was posted by Milo Miller on 30 May 2016 and lists named contributors. It covers acquisition, access, preservation, organization, identity, privacy, subject analysis, and permissions. It is influential, explicitly living/revisable guidance, not law or a current national standard.
- The [Zine cataloging](https://www.zinelibraries.info/running-a-zine-library/zine-cataloging/) resource hub has no single author/date. It identifies Holly Casio and Nicola C. as UK workshop practitioners, links Franklin & Marshall procedures, and points to practitioner scholarship and vocabularies.
- The [Collection Policy Primer](https://www.zinelibraries.info/running-a-zine-library/collection-policy-primer/) was drafted by Milo and Lisa Dee in August 2014 from a Zine Librarians unConference discussion. It recommends documenting mission, audience, formats, subjects, geography, languages, selection, deaccession, and unsolicited gifts.
- The [Queer Zine Library cataloguing manual](https://docs.google.com/document/d/12KluusaAdw7Ao5cYXK6sxA1Okm-0anghvWVpVb7fwjs/edit) is a living volunteer procedure from QZL, London, adapted from RDA, ZineCore, and the Code of Ethics. It gives detailed local rules for names, titles, tags, descriptions, dates, physical description, comments, cover images, and takedowns.
- [Barnard Zine Library](https://zines.barnard.edu/about-collection) and [Access & Circulation](https://zines.barnard.edu/collection-access-circulation) document an institutional collection with open stacks, special collections, catalog discovery, local call numbers, and copyright/access distinctions.
- [ABC No Rio](https://exile.abcnorio.org/facilities/zine-library/) documents a volunteer-run community collection founded in 1998 after rescuing the Blackout Zine Library, with Lower East Side, downtown Manhattan, counterculture, and DIY movement scope.
- [ZineCat/Zine Union Catalog](https://www.zinelibraries.info/zine-union-catalog/) documents a shared discovery project across heterogeneous schemas and vocabularies, with xZINECOREx described as a Dublin Core-based starting point.
- The [ZLuC archive and organizers toolkit](https://www.zinelibraries.info/events/zine-librarian-unconferences/zluc-organizers-toolkit/) document peer problem-solving, accessibility, safer spaces, collaborative notes, and consent around recording, photography, and attribution.

The [Zine Libraries cataloguing resource](https://www.zinelibraries.info/running-a-zine-library/zine-cataloging/) also identifies *Cutter and Paste* (Freedman and Kauffman, 2013), Berthoud (2018), and O'Dell (2014). Their relevance is verified there; detailed guidance is not attributed without full-text review.

## 2. What the evidence agrees on

### 2.1 Description must preserve material and social context

Piepmeier, Triggs, Zavam, and the practitioner guidance all reject a record that
reduces a zine to title, creator, and year. A zine's folds, collage, handwriting,
photocopying, visual composition, irregular pagination, workshop context, scene,
or circulation route may be part of what it communicates. Baker and Cantillon
add that zine-making may be both an archival product and an archival process.

The operational consequence is not to put every observation into a single long
abstract. It is to separate form/genre, subject, physical evidence, production
event, community context, description, and provenance, then link them.

### 2.2 Creator autonomy is a cataloguing requirement

The Code of Ethics and QZL agree on a conservative rule: use the name appearing
on the piece; do not play detective; do not expose a legal identity not
explicitly connected by the creator; treat collectives, pseudonyms, and
anonymous authorship as valid public states. A donor, submitter, teacher,
editor, or archive is not automatically the creator.

This is stronger than a generic “name authority” workflow. It requires public
display name, authorship status, role, source, visibility, and any private
identity evidence to be separable. It also requires correction, suppression,
deadname/outing avoidance, and takedown procedures that do not require a
pseudonymous creator to reveal a legal identity.

### 2.3 Controlled vocabularies are useful but not sufficient

The Code recommends established vocabularies such as LCSH, LCGFT, AAT, and the
Anchor Archive Subject Thesaurus, while retaining creator/community language.
QZL's local practice checks existing tags, limits tag volume, consults
Homosaurus, Anchor Archive, AAT, and LCSH, and discusses new terms collectively.
Drabinski explains why this is not merely a temporary vocabulary gap: a
controlled vocabulary cannot be corrected once and for all to become neutral.

The agreement is therefore dual: use normalized access points for collocation,
but preserve the original term, mapping, vocabulary version, source, and
history. A translation or controlled subject must not silently replace a
Portuguese or community term.

### 2.4 Access is multidimensional

Interference Archive's practice, Barnard's open-stack/special-collection split,
the Code of Ethics, and QZL's takedown procedures all support separate states
for discovery, reading, lending, download, preservation, replication, and reuse.
An openly listed record is not necessarily a public scan. A readable file is not
necessarily licensed for mirroring, commercial reuse, or derivative works. An
anti-copyright or “please copy” statement should be preserved as a creator
statement, not treated as a blanket legal or operational authorization.

### 2.5 Provenance and uncertainty must remain visible

QZL brackets supplied titles and estimated dates and records where a value came
from a distro, website, or email. The book examples include missing title and
copyright pages and bracketed publisher/place data. ZineCat and Barnard document
work-in-progress or unprocessed material. The evidence consistently favors
explicit unknown, estimated, supplied, observed, imported, and unresolved states
over clean-looking guesses.

## 3. Points of tension or disagreement

The sources do not disagree about the need for care, but they embody different
priorities and operating constraints.

### 3.1 Collocation versus creator language

Library systems need normalized terms and searchable access points; creator and
community practice warns that normalization can erase or expose. QZL's limited
tags and collective review favor a deliberately small, local vocabulary. ZineCat
and institutional catalogues favor shared searchable fields across collections.
The defensible resolution for Biblioteca is not choosing one side: retain both
the source term and the mapped term, expose the mapping only at the chosen
visibility level, and version the decision.

### 3.2 Completeness versus privacy

More detail improves discovery and historical research, but the Code explicitly
warns that more detail can expose living people. A required legal name, exact
place, full description, cover image, or searchable OCR may improve a catalogue
while increasing harm. Biblioteca should therefore treat `unknown`, `withheld`,
`private`, `anonymous`, and `unresolved` as valid states, not data-quality
failures.

### 3.3 Open access versus creator control

Interference Archive values open stacks and participatory interpretation, while
Barnard and the Code document restrictions and creator permission around
reproduction. This is a policy tension, not an inconsistency to solve with one
boolean. The same publication may be discoverable, readable in a controlled
setting, preserved internally, and unavailable for public download or reuse.

### 3.4 Standardization versus local capacity

ZineCore2, ZineCat, and Dublin Core-style crosswalks enable interoperability.
QZL's living manual and ZLuC's peer network show that local volunteer capacity,
community trust, and specific collections shape workable rules. Biblioteca should
export to shared profiles, but keep richer local provenance and governance rather
than making the local database conform to the least expressive interchange
format.

### 3.5 “Zine” as a category versus historically situated forms

Duncombe, Triggs, Sabin/Triggs, and Zavam support treating zine, fanzine,
alternative comics, literary fanzine, and related DIY forms as connected but not
interchangeable. A creator's term, a catalogue form term, a subject, and a
collection scope should remain distinct.

## 4. What ZineCore2 contributes

[ZineCore2](https://zinecore.org/) is the clearest current interoperability
reference in the reviewed material. Its documentation describes four related
Dublin Core application profiles, each with narrative documentation, a DC TAP
table, JSON Schema, JSON-LD context, and TypeScript types:

- [ZineCore2](https://zinecore.org/docs/specification/profiles/zinecore2) describes an issue and supports repeatable series, creators, contributors, publishers, dates, languages, rights, identifiers, relations, issue designation, and edition statement.
- [AgentCore2](https://zinecore.org/docs/specification/profiles/agentcore2) makes public display name primary and provides agent kind, roles, alternative names, pseudonyms, anonymity/privacy, and optional private legal/contact values.
- [HoldingCore2](https://zinecore.org/docs/specification/profiles/holdingcore2) separates a described zine from a repository-specific physical or digital holding, including location, call number, condition, access status, URL, and digitization/duplication status.
- [RepoCore2](https://zinecore.org/docs/specification/profiles/repocore2) separates repository identity, scope, location, identifiers, status, and access policy from publications and agents.

These separations are valuable for Biblioteca. They prevent a title record from
being confused with a particular copy or file; make roles repeatable; support
series/issue/edition relationships; and provide a shape for exports and
cross-system comparison. ZineCore2's public [specification repository](https://github.com/ZineCore2/spec)
and [reference server](https://github.com/ZineCore2/server) also demonstrate a
practical machine-readable ecosystem.

ZineCore2 is not, however, a complete answer for Biblioteca:

- its required fields should not become required intake facts when the evidence
  is unknown or withheld;
- its public documentation, TAP, schema, types, and server were not fully
  synchronized in the reviewed snapshot, and the repositories used a `develop`
  branch with no releases then visible;
- a field or JSON Schema does not prove data quality, custody, rights, or
  preservation;
- a `public` boolean is not enough for field- and relationship-level privacy;
- flat source, relation, rights, date, and identifier arrays need richer local
  assertions carrying role, scope, source, confidence, date, and visibility;
- ZineCore2 does not by itself establish Brazilian Portuguese terminology, UF
  normalization, creator review, or local community governance.

Professional practice goes beyond ZineCore2 in at least six ways: conservative
identity handling; creator-supplied descriptions; explicit takedown and
correction; community review of terms; distinction between creator copying
requests and legal/operational permission; and preservation of the reason and
history behind each sensitive or normalized assertion.

Biblioteca should therefore use ZineCore2-shaped exports and concepts, not treat
the current public artifacts as its immutable database contract. Pin an exact
reviewed version for any future crosswalk and document information lost in the
export.

## 5. Brazil-specific implications

### 5.1 The evidence is useful but heterogeneous

The Brazilian evidence consists of different kinds of objects and cannot be
combined into a national standard:

- [Biblioteca de Zines](https://www.biblioteca-de-zines.com.br/) provides a
  strong reader-facing baseline: title, author/collective link, year, category,
  short description, cover/thumbnail, and reading link, with search, year, and
  category filters. The checked public record does not expose physical extent,
  dimensions, binding/assembly, reproduction method, country, or UF as stable
  record fields. These are display facts, not proof of every internal field.
- [La Fanzinoteca's Brazil index](https://fanzinoteca.net/fanzine-country/brasil/)
  and records such as [Papel Xerocado Manifesto Cultural 1](https://fanzinoteca.net/fanzine/papel-xerocado-manifesto-cultural-1/)
  and [Minizine](https://fanzinoteca.net/fanzine/minizine/) visibly separate an
  entry/holding identifier, authorship, language/date, dimensions, reproduction,
  broad binding category, content/category, and a Brazil tag. This is strong
  evidence that those distinctions are useful, not evidence of a complete
  Brazilian schema. A country tag is not place created, published, or UF.
- The [Zine Gororoba link hub](https://linktr.ee/zinegororoba), linked by
  Biblioteca, points to a Curitiba art-zine initiative with editions,
  workshops/events, prompts, themes, and digital links. It supports modeling
  series, event, place, and community context, but does not establish
  university-wide policy, custody, rights, or stable physical metadata.
- [Marca de Fantasia](https://marcadefantasia.com/) demonstrates publisher,
  creator/editor, section/series, cover, persistent page, and publisher context.
  Its Paraíba location is publisher context, not automatically the place of
  publication of every work.
- Zavam's full-text study provides Brazilian conceptual evidence that form,
  language, production, position, and circulation are related. Zauith, Davanco,
  and Barbieri provide a Brazilian workshop case in which participants and
  production context matter. Silva's UnB thesis is a valuable Portuguese-
  language lead, but its detailed proposals remain unverified.

### 5.2 Place and UF must be role-qualified

Brazilian records should preserve the source wording and separately store a
normalized municipality, country, and `state_uf` when supported. Recommended
place roles include `created-in`, `published-in`, `printed-in`, `distributed-in`,
`circulated-in`, `event/workshop`, `creator-context`, `publisher-location`, and
`holding-location`.

Do not infer UF from an ambiguous city, a creator's current address, a country
tag, a publisher/archive location, or the location of Biblioteca. Do not infer
Portuguese from a Brazilian website, title, or publisher. Record language from
the publication or a reliable source, and preserve Portuguese creator/community
terms beside any English or controlled-vocabulary mapping.

### 5.3 Production and community context are often central

For Brazilian workshop, university, neighborhood, collective, and event-based
zines, record the production project or event as a linked context with its own
visibility and consent state. A workshop facilitator is not automatically a
creator. One project may yield multiple individually described zines while
retaining the project relationship.

## 6. Recommended Biblioteca model and fields

Unknown, withheld, private, unresolved, and not-yet-processed are valid states.
They should not be silently converted to empty strings or guessed values.

### 6.1 Required public acceptance core

| Field | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| `biblioteca_id` | Stable local identifier. Keep external IDs separate. |
| `title_as_presented` | Preserve spelling, punctuation, capitalization, diacritics, and creator wording. |
| `record_status` | At minimum distinguish draft, accepted, unprocessed, withdrawn, suppressed, and unresolved. |
| `public_display_name` or explicit attribution state | Use the name presented by the creator/publication, or `anonymous`, `unknown`, `private`, or `unresolved` where appropriate. |
| `provenance` | Every accepted supplied, observed, imported, normalized, or unresolved claim has source, actor/method, date, and verification state. |
| `visibility` | Scope public, limited, internal, suppressed, or delayed visibility to the field or relationship, not only the record. |

### 6.2 Publication, people, and description

| Field | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| `normalized_search_title`, `alternative_title` | Optional, repeatable, source-qualified access points that do not replace the presented title. |
| `agent` + `agent_role` | Repeatable typed relationships for creator, co-creator, editor, illustrator, publisher, distributor, submitter, facilitator, participant, documentation author, and archive steward. Include order, authorship status, source, confidence, and visibility. |
| `series`, `issue_designation`, `edition_statement` | Keep series, issue number/season/unnumbered state, reprint, corrected, translated, or special edition distinct. Do not infer issue from year. |
| `form_genre` | Optional but strongly recommended. Keep zine/fanzine/perzine/comic zine/single-sheet and similar terms distinct from subject. Preserve vocabulary/version/source. |
| `subject` | Repeatable topical terms, with creator/local wording beside normalized mappings. |
| `description_creator` | Creator-supplied wording or marked transcription, with language, source, and visibility. |
| `description_cataloguer` | Separate summary with author, date, evidence, and public-display decision. |
| `language_content` | Optional repeatable language values, including multilingual, non-textual, and unknown states. Do not infer from title or country. |
| `date_statement` and normalized date assertions | Preserve “circa,” season, range, undated, or supplied wording. Add role, precision, uncertainty, and source for normalized values. |

### 6.3 Place, materiality, and delivery

| Field | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| `place_assertion` | Repeatable place plus role, original wording, precision, source, and visibility. |
| `municipality`, `country`, `state_uf` | Optional normalized values only when the source supports the relationship. UF applies only to supported Brazilian place values. |
| `community_context` | Workshop, event, educational setting, scene, venue, collective, neighborhood, or circulation network, linked rather than forced into subject. |
| `physical_extent` | Page count, sheet count, volume/copy count, and counting convention; distinguish supplied, measured, and observed values. |
| `dimensions` | Width, height, unit, and state such as folded/closed, open, or trimmed; retain supplied versus measured. |
| `binding_assembly` | Repeatable observations such as folded, stapled, sewn, loose sheets, accordion, insert, or other; preserve unknown. |
| `reproduction_method` | Photocopy, risograph, offset, digital print, handwritten, screenprint, or other only when creator-supplied, catalogued, or observed. |
| `material_features` | Collage, handwriting, paper, unusual pagination, inserts, color, altered material, legibility, and other evidence. |
| `delivery_format` | Physical printed, digital-born, scan, web publication, audio/video, or mixed. Never use it as a substitute for physical form. |

### 6.4 Holdings, assets, access, rights, and governance

| Field | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| `holding` | Repository/steward, collection, accession, call number, location, copy/condition, custody evidence, and processing status. |
| `asset` | File/URL kind, source, format, checksum when available, scan properties, version, derivative relation, and ambiguous-match state. |
| `access_decisions` | Separate discovery, reading, download, lending, preservation, replication, and reuse; scope each to publication, issue, copy, file, page, or derivative. |
| `rights_claim` | Preserve original wording, claimant/source, date, scope, confidence, and normalized mapping. `unknown` means unknown. |
| `creator_copying_request` | Preserve “please copy,” anti-copyright, copyleft, or similar language separately from legal or operational permission. |
| `consent_correction_withdrawal` | Record request, decision, date, scope, evidence, and responsible reviewer without requiring legal identity disclosure. |
| `vocabulary_version` | Record vocabulary identifier/version, preferred label, local mapping, historical term, source, and review history. |
| `external_identifier` | Typed system/value/URI with source, object type, verification, and visibility. |
| `table_of_contents` | Deferred until Biblioteca has a transcription and scope policy; do not require it for image-led or non-paginated zines. |

## 7. Workflow and governance

### Intake and triage

1. Accept the smallest truthful public record: local ID, presented title,
   public attribution state, provenance, record status, and visibility.
2. Keep submission data distinct from catalogued publication data. A submitter
   is not automatically creator or rights-holder.
3. Create publication/issue, agent, context, holding, and asset records only
   where the evidence supports them. Permit a publication to exist before a
   physical copy or file is verified.
4. Mark evidence as creator-supplied, cataloguer-observed, imported,
   cataloguer-inferred, or unresolved. Inferred values should not be silently
   presented as facts.

### Cataloguing and review

1. Transcribe the title and public name from the publication where possible.
2. Use creator description first; add a cataloguer summary as a named,
   separate intervention.
3. Describe the physical copy directly. Keep PDF page count and physical page
   count on separate targets.
4. Add controlled terms only with a mapping source and vocabulary version.
   Preserve local Portuguese and creator terms.
5. Route contested names, sensitive identities, new local tags, and community
   descriptions to peer or community review. Record the decision without
   publishing private deliberation.
6. Expose a visible correction, suppression, withdrawal, and takedown route.
   Review related records when a name or term is removed.

### Publication and maintenance

1. Generate the public catalogue as a projection from richer internal
   assertions, not as the only place where uncertainty and privacy decisions
   exist.
2. Make web indexing, cover display, OCR, download, and reuse independently
   configurable.
3. Version policies and vocabularies. Record who changed a sensitive assertion,
   when, why, and on what evidence.
4. Export a ZineCore2-shaped projection only after pinning the reviewed artifact
   version and documenting lost semantics.
5. Test the model with synthetic cases: collective, pseudonymous creator,
   anonymous creator, bilingual issue, uncertain date, Brazilian municipality
   and UF, folded sheets with an insert, unknown rights, physical holding,
   externally sourced scan, and ambiguous asset match.

Governance should name who may approve public attribution, who can restrict or
remove a record, who reviews vocabulary mappings, how creator requests are
handled, and how policy changes are logged. A community-informed process is not
the same as claiming that Biblioteca owns or speaks for the community.

## 8. What should not be inferred

Biblioteca should explicitly prohibit the following shortcuts:

- A PDF, cover thumbnail, filename, image aspect ratio, or URL does not prove
  physical existence, page count, dimensions, binding, assembly, color, or
  reproduction method.
- A digital PDF page count is not physical pagination unless a source establishes
  that relationship.
- Visual appearance does not prove photocopy, risograph, offset, handwriting,
  or any other production method.
- A catalogue record does not prove a physical holding; a physical holding does
  not prove a scan; a scan does not prove an authorized original or preservation
  master.
- A country tag, archive location, publisher location, creator address, or
  collection scope does not prove place created, place published, or Brazilian
  UF.
- A Brazilian website or title does not prove Portuguese content language.
- A subject heading, keyword, collection name, or event does not automatically
  become a genre, creator, publisher, or place assertion.
- A workshop article's authors are not automatically the creators of each zine
  described by the article.
- A submitter, donor, editor, teacher, distributor, publisher, archive, or
  researcher is not automatically the author or rights-holder.
- A public record, free reading link, anti-copyright phrase, or catalogue-site
  licence does not automatically authorize downloading, mirroring, digitizing,
  republishing, creating derivatives, or commercial reuse of the underlying
  zine.
- “Anonymous,” “pseudonymous,” “private,” “unknown,” and “unresolved” are not
  interchangeable and should not be resolved by web searching.
- A collection count, search facet, partner listing, or catalogue presence does
  not prove completeness, currentness, or universal coverage.
- ZineCore2 fields, ZineCat fields, Barnard's call numbers, QZL's local rules,
  La Fanzinoteca's values, or a publisher's practice do not constitute a
  universal Brazilian zine standard.

## 9. Final assessment

The academic and practitioner evidence points in the same direction: good zine
cataloguing is bibliographically disciplined and socially accountable. It
describes the publication and its material evidence, but also records how the
publication came to the catalogue, who is allowed to be named, which terms were
chosen, what remains unknown, which copy or file is held, and what users may do.

ZineCore2 supplies a valuable structural baseline for interoperability. The
professional zine-librarian tradition supplies the cautions that a schema alone
cannot supply: do not investigate creators, do not erase local language, do not
confuse access with permission, and make description revisable by affected
people. Brazilian sources add a strong need for Portuguese-preserving terms,
role-qualified geography, municipality/UF caution, and explicit production and
community context. They do not justify claiming that one Brazilian schema has
already been established.

For Biblioteca, the right implementation is therefore not maximal required
metadata. It is a layered, provenance-rich, privacy-aware catalogue with a
small truthful public core, optional evidence-based material fields, separate
holdings and assets, action-specific access decisions, and governance capable of
correction, withdrawal, and vocabulary change.
